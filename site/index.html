Every job from 800+ company career sites, scored against your own skills, on your machine.

Job-I reads the employers' own job pages and the boards you pick, puts every posting in one list, and ranks it by the skills you say you have and the skills you want to use. It runs on your computer. There is no account and nothing to sign up for.

The tools

Six screens, one list of postings under all of them. Every screenshot here comes from a made-up demo profile.

Jobs

One list from every source. When the same vacancy is on the company site and on two boards, it shows up once, with the other places to apply listed under it.

Each posting is read for the skills it asks for. You give every skill two numbers from 0 to 10: My expertise (how much of it you can show) and Goal importance (how much the job you want needs it). That gives every posting two scores, fit and goal, and the card shows their average. A posting that asks for three things you rate 9 beats one that asks for twenty you don't.

Narrow the list by profession, field, skill, country, work type, workload and posting date. Rules drop postings that have a word you name in the title or the description.

No model picks your jobs. The scoring is plain arithmetic, so the same postings and the same profile always give the same list.

ATS check

Every posting has a CV check. It reads your actual CV file (Word, PDF or Markdown) and shows:

  • which skills from the posting your CV mentions, weighted by how much the posting needs each one, with a letter grade from A to D
  • skills that appear only in a skills list and never in your work history
  • whether the job title appears anywhere in your CV, because recruiters search by title
  • file checks that resume parsers document: contact details, section headings, date ranges

This shows what keyword matching sees. It does not predict an outcome. An applicant tracking system (ATS) doesn't reject a CV on its content by default; the automatic rejections come from screening questions.

Markets

Every posting Job-I has ever pulled is kept, with the day it first appeared and the day it disappeared. The Markets tab uses that history to show:

  • how many jobs are posted over time
  • which skills employers ask for, next to your own two numbers
  • who is hiring
  • how long roles stay open
  • salaries where postings publish them, plus the split by work mode and country

Filter by profession, field or country, or look at the whole market.

Applied

Mark a job as applied and it moves to a timeline. Marking one copy covers every other place the same vacancy was posted. Attach the CV and cover letter you sent; Job-I keeps a copy as it was that day, so editing your CV later doesn't change the record.

If you connect your mailbox, Job-I reads it over IMAP with an app password you create and matches replies to the applications they answer. You can see who replied, who turned you down, and who has been quiet for how many days. The mailbox is off until you set it up.

Documents

Your CVs, cover letters and other files, in folders next to the app. Drop files in, paste them, or upload them. Word, OpenDocument and PowerPoint files open in the program your computer already uses for them. The ATS check reads CVs from here.

Companies

The company list ships with the app: every employer Job-I knows, where it is, and how to pull its jobs. Each one shows facts from public sources, labelled with where they came from: kununu and Glassdoor ratings, founding year and headcount from Wikidata, its public GitHub organisation, and Great Place to Work entries.

The list also says which employers can't be pulled yet, and why. Add a company yourself and Job-I looks up the same facts for it.

Your data stays on your computer

Job-I is one file. Put it in a folder, run it, and everything it creates goes in that folder: your profile, your CVs, your applications, the postings it pulled. Back it up, move it to another computer or delete it. There is no Job-I server and no account. Nothing about you is sent to us.

No telemetry

No analytics, no crash reports, no usage counts. This website has no tracking either.

What it connects to

  • Career sites and job boards, to fetch postings when you press Sync. Your profile is never sent to them; scoring happens after the download, on your machine.
  • GitHub only, for updates: once a day it reads a small version file from raw.githubusercontent.com. Newer company lists and skill vocabulary come from there automatically; a new version of the app is downloaded from github.com only when you click Install. Everything is signed and checked before it's used.
  • Your own mailbox, only if you set it up.
  • Public company sources (kununu, Glassdoor through Bing, Wikidata, GitHub), only when you add a company or refresh its facts.
  • Claude Code, only if you turn it on: an optional check after Sync, for sources that broke, runs through your own Claude Code install and account. It is off by default, and Claude Code being installed doesn't turn it on.

Sharing is opt-in

A coming "Share my changes" button will send fixes to the shared company list and skill dictionary back to this project. It sends only the items you tick, after you've seen them. Your profile, CVs and applications are never part of it.

First launch

Put the file in an empty folder of its own, such as Job-I. When it starts, it creates its data next to itself and opens the dashboard. The files aren't code-signed yet, so your system will warn you the first time:

Windows

SmartScreen says "Windows protected your PC". Click More info, then Run anyway.

macOS

In Terminal, from the folder:

chmod +x job-i-darwin-arm64
xattr -d com.apple.quarantine job-i-darwin-arm64
./job-i-darwin-arm64

Or, in Finder, right-click the file and choose Open. On macOS 15 and later, try opening it once, then go to System Settings, Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. On an Intel Mac, use job-i-darwin-x64.

Linux

chmod +x job-i-linux-x64
./job-i-linux-x64

After that, Job-I checks for updates itself and shows new versions in its notification bell. It never installs one without asking.
